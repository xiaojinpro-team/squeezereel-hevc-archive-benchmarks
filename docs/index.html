HEVC archive benchmarks for ProRes 4444

Run by the SqueezeReel team while building its archive lanes. Full tables, method and scripts: GitHub repository. The same results on the web: English, 中文. Product: archive.

Sources: ARRI ProRes 4444 footage from a studio green-screen project (3840x2160, 25 fps, 12-bit 4:4:4, about 880 Mbps) and ProRes 4444 clips with a real alpha channel. Tools: ffmpeg 9.0.2, x265 4.3, VideoToolbox.

TL;DR

  1. 4:4:4 chroma gave no measurable chroma benefit. Chroma PSNR/SSIM of x265 4:4:4 and VideoToolbox 4:2:2 are within 0.25 dB, even on a saturated red backdrop. 4:4:4 wins on luma (+0.9 to +1.5 dB PSNR, VMAF about +1.2) and keying edges (36% to 47% fewer matte edge mismatches), at 7 to 14 times the file size.
  2. x265 medium matches slower on matte quality and is about 10 times faster (3 to 3.8 fps against 0.35 fps on an M4 Mac mini), at about 60% of the size. VMAF minimum is 0.4 to 0.7 lower.
  3. 12-bit (main444-12) is not worth making the default: 26% to 32% more bytes, 5% to 27% slower, +0.15 dB luma PSNR.
  4. HEVC with alpha works on macOS. VideoToolbox with alpha_quality 1.0 kept the alpha plane bit-exact on both test clips at about 12 times compression. Apple's avconvert preset reaches about 22 times, but soft-edge alpha deviates by up to 37 to 42/255.
  5. libx265 cannot write an alpha layer in the x265 4.3 build tested.
